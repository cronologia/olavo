José Ortega y Gasset (1883–1955)

Spanish philosopher, essayist and intellectual leader, formed in German neo-Kantianism and creator of 'vital reason' ('I am I and my circumstance'). Professor of metaphysics in Madrid and founder of the Revista de Occidente, he became world-famous with The Revolt of the Masses. He left Spain with the Civil War and returned after the Second World War.[3] [4]

Life and works

YearEvent
9 May 1883Born in Madrid, into a family tied to liberal journalism.[3] [4]
c. 1905–1907 ≈Studies in Germany — Leipzig, Berlin and, decisively, Marburg, under neo-Kantianism — before returning to Madrid.[3]
1910Wins the competition (8 October) for the chair of metaphysics at the Central University of Madrid, vacant since the death of Nicolás Salmerón.[3]
1914Publishes Meditaciones del Quijote, where the formula 'I am I and my circumstance' appears.[3] [4]
1921/1922Publishes España invertebrada — the SEP dates the book to 1921; Britannica, to 1922.[3] [4]
1923Founds the Revista de Occidente, the leading organ of intellectual renewal in the Spain of the period.[4]
1929/1930Publishes La rebelión de las masas, his most famous work — Britannica dates it to 1929 (as a series of articles); the SEP records the book in 1930.[4] [3]
1931Becomes one of the first members of the Constituent Cortes of the Second Spanish Republic.[3]
1936–1945With the outbreak of the Civil War, leaves Spain; lives in Europe and Argentina, returning at the end of the Second World War (Britannica describes the period as voluntary exile).[3] [4]
1948Founds in Madrid, with his disciple Julián Marías, the Instituto de Humanidades — he never resumed his university chair, and the government restricts the institute's activities until its closure in 1950.[3]
18 October 1955Dies in Madrid.[4] [3]
