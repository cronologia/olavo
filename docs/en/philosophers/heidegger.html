Heidegger

Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)

A central figure of twentieth-century European philosophy, author of Being and Time (1927) and renewer of the question of the meaning of being. Husserl's disciple and successor at Freiburg, he influenced existentialism, hermeneutics and deconstruction. His involvement with Nazism — the 1933–34 rectorate and party membership until 1945 — remains an object of controversy.[3] [4] [5]

Life and works

YearEvent
26 September 1889Born in Messkirch, in the Black Forest region of Germany.[4] [3]
1923Becomes associate professor at the University of Marburg, where he teaches ontology, logic and the history of philosophy until 1928.[3] [4]
1927Publishes Being and Time, his greatest work, and is promoted to full professor at Marburg.[3] [4]
1928Appointed to the Freiburg chair previously held by Rickert and by Husserl, whose successor he becomes.[3]
April 1933Elected rector of the University of Freiburg after Hitler's seizure of power; joins the Nazi Party on 1 May and conducts the 'alignment' (Gleichschaltung) of the university.[3]
April 1934Resigns the rectorate, but remains a party member until the end of the regime.[3]
1936–1939Delivers the great lecture courses on Nietzsche, later collected in book form; in the 1930s–40s he also lectures on Hölderlin, Schelling, Hegel and the pre-Socratics.[3]
1945–1946After Germany's surrender and the denazification hearings, the French military government bans him from all teaching and university activity.[3]
1949The teaching ban is lifted, allowing him to assume emeritus status at Freiburg.[3]
1950Publishes Holzwege (Off the Beaten Track), collecting essays from 1935–1946.[3]
26 May 1976Dies in Freiburg im Breisgau.[4]
