Dante Alighieri

Dante Alighieri (Durante di Alighiero degli Alighieri) (c. 1265–1321)

Poet, political thinker and 'father of the Italian language', author of the Divine Comedy, the poetic synthesis of medieval culture. He took an active part in the civic life of Florence until his condemnation to exile in 1302, and wrote in banishment his great prose works and the poem that immortalized him. He died in Ravenna in 1321.[3] [4]
