Karl Marx (1818–1883)

German philosopher, economist and revolutionary, author (with Friedrich Engels) of the Communist Manifesto and of Capital. His theory of history, his critique of political economy and the political movement he inspired profoundly marked the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.[1] [2]

Life and works

YearEvent
5 May 1818Born in Trier, in the Rhine province of Prussia.[2]
1835–1836Enters the University of Bonn (October 1835) and transfers the following year to Berlin, where he studies law and philosophy.[2]
April 1841Takes his doctorate in philosophy from the University of Jena.[2]
October 1842Becomes editor of the Rheinische Zeitung in Cologne.[2]
1843Marries Jenny von Westphalen (June) and moves to Paris months later.[2]
February 1845Expelled from France, settles in Brussels, followed by Engels; from this period date the manuscripts of The German Ideology (1845–46), published posthumously.[2] [1]
1848Publishes the Communist Manifesto with Engels, in the year of the European revolutions.[2] [1]
August 1849Expelled from Paris once more, settles in London, his home for the rest of his life.[3]
1859Publishes A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy, whose Preface carries the classic formulation of his materialist conception of history.[1]
1864Foundation of the International Working Men's Association (First International); neither its founder nor its head, Marx becomes its leading intellectual figure, drafting the inaugural address and the provisional statutes.[4]
1867Publishes volume I of Capital (volumes II and III would appear posthumously, in 1885 and 1894, edited by Engels).[5]
14 March 1883Dies in London.[2]
