Aristotle (384–322 BC)

Greek philosopher, disciple of Plato and tutor of Alexander of Macedon, founder of the Lyceum in Athens. His surviving treatises cover logic, physics, biology, metaphysics, ethics, politics, rhetoric and poetics, and shaped much of ancient, medieval and modern philosophy.[1] [2] [3]

Life and works

YearEvent
384 BCBorn in Stagira, a small city of Chalcidice in northern Greece.[1] [3]
367 BCMoves to Athens and enters Plato's Academy, where he remains for about twenty years as disciple and colleague.[1] [3]
347 BCOn Plato's death, leaves Athens and settles in Assos, in Asia Minor, with the ruler Hermias.[1]
c. 347–343 BC ≈In Assos and then on the island of Lesbos, pursues research in marine biology; marries Pythias, Hermias's niece.[1]
343 BCSummoned by Philip II to the Macedonian court at Pella, becomes tutor of the king's son, the future Alexander the Great (Britannica records 343 or 342 BC).[1] [3]
335 BCReturns to Athens and founds his own school, the Lyceum, by the gymnasium dedicated to Apollo Lyceus.[1] [3]
c. 335–323 BC ≈The Lyceum period: composition and organization of most of the surviving corpus — among others the Organon (logic), the Physics, the Metaphysics, the Nicomachean Ethics, the Politics and the Poetics; the internal dating of the treatises is uncertain and debated by specialists.[1] [2]
323 BCOn Alexander's death and the anti-Macedonian reaction in Athens, withdraws from the city to Chalcis, on the island of Euboea.[1] [3]
322 BCDies in Chalcis, Euboea.[1] [3]

How he read him

Reception, not philosophy: each entry records how Olavo de Carvalho characterised this philosopher, attributed and dated to the 2002 course. The project does not adjudicate who is right. The recordings are machine-transcribed and unchecked against audio, so everything here is paraphrase and nothing is quoted.

  • The whole session insists on the order of the corpus: Aristotle begins as a teacher of rhetoric inside the Academy, and rhetoric already had centuries of tradition behind it from the sophists — whose real discovery, he says, was that two people arguing form a single piece of reasoning between them.

    Curso História Essencial da Filosofia, session 04 — Aristotle — 2002 ?

  • A reception history in one line: Aristotle only began to be understood in the twentieth century, with two or three exceptions. Aquinas's readings are brilliant, but he is not always talking about Aristotle — sometimes he is inventing another philosophy of his own.

    Curso História Essencial da Filosofia, session 04 — Aristotle — 2002 ?

  • On Werner Jaeger's intellectual biography — a brilliant book whose thesis is wrong. Jaeger has Aristotle moving from strict Platonism to modern empiricism; a century of argument concluded no such transition happened. But both poles really are in the corpus, so a reader has to live with the metaphysician and the empiricist at once rather than dissolving the problem.

    Curso História Essencial da Filosofia, session 04 — Aristotle — 2002 ?

  • He mentions an earlier course of his own in Rio, Pensamento e Atualidade de Aristóteles, of which some twelve lectures were nothing but the history of misreadings of Aristotle.

    Curso História Essencial da Filosofia, session 04 — Aristotle — 2002 ?
