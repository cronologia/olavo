Eric Voegelin

Eric Hermann Wilhelm Voegelin (1901–1985)

German-American political philosopher, formed in Vienna, who fled Nazism in 1938 and made his career in the United States and Munich. He sought a comprehensive philosophy of man, society and history, and became known for his analysis of modern political movements as 'gnosticism'. His principal works include The New Science of Politics and the Order and History series.[3] [4]

Life and works

3 January 1901Born in Cologne, Germany; the family later moves to Vienna, where he receives his entire formation.[3] [4]
1928Obtains the habilitation and begins teaching at the University of Vienna (Britannica records his teaching at the Law Faculty from 1929 to 1938).[4] [3]
1933Publishes two books critically analyzing racism and race ideas, which set him against Nazism.[4]
1936Publishes Der autoritäre Staat (The Authoritarian State).[3]
1938After the Anschluss, flees Austria with his wife, passing through Switzerland before emigrating to the United States.[3] [4]
1942–1958Professor at Louisiana State University, Baton Rouge; becomes a US citizen in 1944.[4]
1952Publishes The New Science of Politics, the work that projects him into the American intellectual debate.[3] [4]
1956–1957Publishes the first three volumes of Order and History; volume 4 appears in 1974 and the posthumous volume 5 in 1987.[4] [3]
1958Takes up in Munich the former chair of Max Weber in political science at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität.[3] [4]
1966Publishes Anamnesis: Zur Theorie der Geschichte und Politik, central to his philosophy of consciousness.[4]
1969Returns to the United States as senior research fellow at the Hoover Institution, Stanford University.[3] [4]
19 January 1985Dies at Stanford, California.[3] [4]
