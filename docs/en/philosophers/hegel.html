Hegel

Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel (1770–1831)

German philosopher, the leading exponent of post-Kantian German idealism. He elaborated a system spanning logic, philosophy of nature and philosophy of spirit, with lasting influence on philosophy, political theory and the historiography of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.[1] [2] [3]

Life and works

YearEvent
27 August 1770Born in Stuttgart, in the duchy of Württemberg.[3] [1]
1788–1793Studies at the Protestant seminary of Tübingen (the Stift), where he forms friendships with the poet Hölderlin and the philosopher Schelling.[1]
1801Moves to Jena, joining Schelling, and publishes his first philosophical work (on the difference between Fichte's and Schelling's systems), along with the dissertation on the orbits of the planets.[1]
1807Publishes the Phenomenology of Spirit, completed at Jena.[1] [3]
1812–1816Publishes the Science of Logic, in parts (1812, 1813 and 1816).[1] [3]
1816Returns to university life as professor at Heidelberg.[1]
1817Publishes the Encyclopedia of the Philosophical Sciences, the systematic exposition of his thought, later revised in new editions.[1] [3]
1818Takes the chair of philosophy at the University of Berlin, the most prestigious in the German philosophical world.[1] [3]
1821Publishes in Berlin the Elements of the Philosophy of Right, his major work of political philosophy.[1] [3]
14 November 1831Dies in Berlin, at the height of his fame.[3] [1]
