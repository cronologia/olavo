René Guénon

René Guénon (Abdel Wahed Yahya) (1886–1951)

French metaphysician and writer, the principal formulator of the twentieth-century 'Traditionalist' or 'Perennialist' current. He wrote on Eastern metaphysics, symbolism, initiation and the critique of the modern world; he embraced Islam and lived his last twenty years in Cairo.[1] [2] [3]

Life and works

YearEvent
1886Born in Blois, France; raised in a strict Catholic milieu and educated by Jesuits.[1] [2] [3]
1905After mathematics studies in Paris (Collège Rollin), abandons preparation for the academic examinations.[1]
c. 1906–1912 ≈Frequents the Parisian occultist and neo-spiritualist milieux — the most obscure period of his biography.[1] [2]
June 1909Founds the occultist review La Gnose, which publishes his first writings, already conversant with Vedanta.[1]
1912Marries a devout Catholic; a new phase of withdrawal from the occultist circles and association with Catholic intellectuals (among them Jacques Maritain).[1]
1921Publishes his first book, Introduction générale à l'étude des doctrines hindoues, and the critique Le Théosophisme: histoire d'une pseudo-religion; L'Erreur spirite follows in 1923.[1] [2]
1924Publishes Orient et Occident.[1] [2]
1925Publishes L'Homme et son devenir selon le Vêdânta, his axial work on Vedanta; L'Ésotérisme de Dante is from the same year.[1] [2]
1927Publishes La Crise du monde moderne and Le Roi du monde; Catholic criticism of the latter deepens his estrangement from the Church.[1] [2]
January 1928Sudden death of his wife.[1]
1930Travels to Cairo for a three-month stay and remains until his death; openly embraces Islam, is initiated into the Shadhiliyya Sufi order under the name Abdel Wahed Yahya, and remarries.[1] [2]
1945Publishes Le Règne de la Quantité et les Signes des Temps, considered his masterpiece of the critique of modernity.[1] [2]
1951Dies in Cairo, Egypt.[1] [3]
