Aurelius Augustinus (Augustine of Hippo) (354–430)

Bishop of Hippo and one of the most influential Christian thinkers of all time, the bridge between ancient philosophy and medieval thought. A professor of rhetoric converted to ascetic Christianity in 386, he left more than five million words of surviving writings, among them the Confessions and The City of God.[3] [4] [5]

386–387At the rural retreat of Cassiciacum, near Milan, writes his first philosophical dialogues (Contra Academicos, De beata vita, De ordine).[3]

391Ordained a priest at Hippo Regius (present-day Annaba, Algeria), apparently against his own wishes.[3] [4]
395/396Becomes bishop of Hippo — Britannica gives 395 or 396; the SEP, about 396.[3] [4]
c. 396–400 ≈Writes the Confessions, 'philosophy in autobiography' — the SEP dates the work to c. 396–400; Britannica, to c. 400.[3] [4]
c. 413–426 ≈Writes The City of God, his great work of the theology of history, begun after the sack of Rome.[4]
