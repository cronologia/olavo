Nietzsche

Friedrich Wilhelm Nietzsche (1844–1900)

German philosopher and cultural critic, trained as a classical philologist, who published intensely through the 1870s and 1880s. He is known for his radical critiques of traditional European morality, religion and philosophy, and for concepts such as the will to power, the eternal recurrence and the Übermensch. His productive career ended abruptly with the mental collapse of 1889.[3] [4] [5]

Life and works

YearEvent
15 October 1844Born in Röcken, near Leipzig, son of a Lutheran pastor.[3] [4]
May 1869At 24, called to the chair of classical philology at the University of Basel — the youngest ever appointed to such a post; in his student years at Leipzig he had met Richard Wagner.[3] [4]
1872Publishes his first book, The Birth of Tragedy, a polemic that breaks with conventional philological scholarship.[3] [4]
1878Publishes Human, All Too Human, the landmark of his 'positivist' phase and of the break with Wagner.[4] [3]
1879Resigns the Basel chair for health reasons; begins the decade of itinerant life between Switzerland, Italy and France, devoted to writing.[3] [4]
1882Publishes The Gay Science (expanded second edition 1887), where the announcement of the 'death of God' first appears.[3]
1883–1885Publishes Thus Spoke Zarathustra, in four parts.[3] [4]
1886–1887Publishes Beyond Good and Evil (1886) and On the Genealogy of Morality (1887), central works of his maturity.[3]
1888Last productive year: writes Twilight of the Idols, The Case of Wagner and other final texts.[3]
January 1889Suffers a mental collapse in Turin; spends his final years in the care of his mother and then his sister, in deepening silence.[3]
25 August 1900Dies in Weimar, of a stroke complicated by pneumonia; his sister Elisabeth takes control of his literary estate.[4] [3]
