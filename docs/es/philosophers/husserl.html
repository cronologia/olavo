Edmund Gustav Albrecht Husserl (1859–1938)

A mathematician by training and the founder of phenomenology, the movement that marked all of twentieth-century continental philosophy. He made his career at Halle, Göttingen and Freiburg, where Heidegger succeeded him. Of Jewish origin, he spent his last years excluded from German university life under the Nazi regime.[3] [4] [5]

Vida y obra

AñoAcontecimiento
8 April 1859Born in Proßnitz, Moravia, then in the Austrian Empire (present-day Prostějov, Czechia).[4]
January 1883Takes his doctorate in mathematics in Vienna with a thesis on the calculus of variations; works briefly as Weierstrass's assistant in Berlin.[3]
1887–1901Privatdozent at the University of Halle for fourteen years; in 1891 publishes his first book, Philosophy of Arithmetic.[3]
1900–1901Publishes the Logical Investigations, the founding work of phenomenology.[3] [4]
1901–1916Professor at Göttingen (extraordinarius from 1901, ordinarius from 1906), a position secured by the success of the Investigations.[3] [4]
1913Publishes Ideas for a Pure Phenomenology (Ideas I), the transcendental turn of his thought.[4]
1916Takes the chair of philosophy at Freiburg, succeeding the neo-Kantian Heinrich Rickert; Edith Stein becomes his assistant.[3]
1928Retires at Freiburg, succeeded by Heidegger; shortly afterwards delivers in Paris the lectures that would become the Cartesian Meditations.[3]
1933With the arrival of Nazism, suffers the exclusion imposed on Jews in Germany — in a letter of 4 May 1933 he speaks of the erection of a 'spiritual ghetto' in which he and his children were being enclosed.[3]
1936Publishes (in part) The Crisis of European Sciences and Transcendental Phenomenology, developed from lectures in Vienna and Prague.[3]
27 April 1938Dies in Freiburg im Breisgau.[4]
