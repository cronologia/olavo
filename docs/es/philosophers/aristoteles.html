Aristotle (384–322 BC)

Greek philosopher, disciple of Plato and tutor of Alexander of Macedon, founder of the Lyceum in Athens. His surviving treatises cover logic, physics, biology, metaphysics, ethics, politics, rhetoric and poetics, and shaped much of ancient, medieval and modern philosophy.[1] [2] [3]

Vida y obra

AñoAcontecimiento
384 BCBorn in Stagira, a small city of Chalcidice in northern Greece.[1] [3]
367 BCMoves to Athens and enters Plato's Academy, where he remains for about twenty years as disciple and colleague.[1] [3]
347 BCOn Plato's death, leaves Athens and settles in Assos, in Asia Minor, with the ruler Hermias.[1]
c. 347–343 BC ≈In Assos and then on the island of Lesbos, pursues research in marine biology; marries Pythias, Hermias's niece.[1]
343 BCSummoned by Philip II to the Macedonian court at Pella, becomes tutor of the king's son, the future Alexander the Great (Britannica records 343 or 342 BC).[1] [3]
335 BCReturns to Athens and founds his own school, the Lyceum, by the gymnasium dedicated to Apollo Lyceus.[1] [3]
c. 335–323 BC ≈The Lyceum period: composition and organization of most of the surviving corpus — among others the Organon (logic), the Physics, the Metaphysics, the Nicomachean Ethics, the Politics and the Poetics; the internal dating of the treatises is uncertain and debated by specialists.[1] [2]
323 BCOn Alexander's death and the anti-Macedonian reaction in Athens, withdraws from the city to Chalcis, on the island of Euboea.[1] [3]
322 BCDies in Chalcis, Euboea.[1] [3]
