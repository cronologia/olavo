Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)

German philosopher, a central figure of modern philosophy, who spent his whole life in Königsberg. His 'critical philosophy', set out in the three Critiques, proposed that human reason legislates both for the knowledge of nature and for morality.[1] [2] [3]

Vida y obra

AñoAcontecimiento
22 April 1724Born in Königsberg, capital of East Prussia.[1] [3]
1740s ≈Studies at the University of Königsberg (the Albertina); after university, works for some years as a private tutor in the region.[1]
1755Takes the degree of Magister and becomes Privatdozent (private lecturer) at Königsberg, a position he holds for fifteen years under a heavy teaching load.[1]
1770Appointed full professor of logic and metaphysics at Königsberg; presents the Inaugural Dissertation, the landmark of his critical turn.[1]
1781Publishes the Critique of Pure Reason (revised 2nd edition 1787).[1] [3]
1783Publishes the Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics.[1]
1785Publishes the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals, his principal work on the fundamental principle of morality.[1]
1788Publishes the Critique of Practical Reason.[1]
1790Publishes the Critique of the Power of Judgment.[1]
1797Publishes The Metaphysics of Morals (Doctrine of Right and Doctrine of Virtue), after Toward Perpetual Peace (1795).[1]
12 February 1804Dies in Königsberg, the city he never left for long.[3]
