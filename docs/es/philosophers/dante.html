Dante Alighieri

Dante Alighieri (Durante di Alighiero degli Alighieri) (c. 1265–1321)

Poet, political thinker and 'father of the Italian language', author of the Divine Comedy, the poetic synthesis of medieval culture. He took an active part in the civic life of Florence until his condemnation to exile in 1302, and wrote in banishment his great prose works and the poem that immortalized him. He died in Ravenna in 1321.[3] [4]

AñoAcontecimiento
c. May–June 1265 ≈Born in Florence — if he was indeed born under Gemini, as the Paradiso suggests, the birthday would fall between mid-May and mid-June (Britannica records c. 21 May–20 June).[3] [4]
c. 1274 ≈By the testimony of his own writings, meets at age nine Beatrice Portinari, whom after her death (1290) he will invoke as the inspiration of his poetic vision.[3]
1285Marries and starts a family; from then on takes part in Florence's cultural and civic life, serves as a soldier and holds various political offices.[3]
c. 1293 ≈Composes the Vita nuova, the prosimetrum in praise of Beatrice.[4]
1302Condemned and exiled from Florence in the struggle between Guelph factions; he will never return to the city.[3] [4]
c. 1304–1307 ≈Writes the Convivio, abandoned with only four of the projected treatises, and De vulgari eloquentia, on the illustrious vernacular.[3]
c. 1308–1321 ≈Composes the Divine Comedy (Inferno, Purgatorio, Paradiso), per Britannica's dating.[4]
1312In the years of the expedition of Henry VII of Luxembourg, crowned emperor in 1312, becomes an impassioned propagandist of universal empire.[3]
after 1314 ≈Writes the Monarchia, the Latin treatise on the necessity of an imperial power independent of the Church — its dating is disputed; the SEP considers composition after 1314 probable.[3]
1319Moves from Verona (the court of Cangrande della Scala) to Ravenna, where he completes the Paradiso.[3]
13/14 September 1321Dies in Ravenna — Britannica records the date as 13/14 September.[4] [3]
