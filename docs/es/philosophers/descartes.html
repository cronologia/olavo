René Descartes (1596–1650)

French philosopher, mathematician and scientist, often described as the founder of modern philosophy. He created analytic geometry, formulated the first modern version of mind–body dualism, and proposed a method of systematic doubt as the foundation of knowledge.[1] [2] [3] [4]

Vida y obra

AñoAcontecimiento
31 March 1596Born in La Haye, in the Touraine region of France.[1] [4]
1606/1607–1614/1615 ≈Studies at the Jesuit college of La Flèche, following the curriculum of grammar, humanities and scholastic philosophy.[1]
1616Takes a degree in law at the University of Poitiers, which he will never practice.[1]
1618Becomes a volunteer soldier at Breda, in the service of Prince Maurice of Nassau; decisive encounter with the scientist Isaac Beeckman.[1]
10 November 1619In the region of Ulm, has the three dreams he interprets as a life mission devoted to the reform of knowledge.[1]
late 1628Abandons the Rules for the Direction of the Mind (unfinished) and settles in the Netherlands, where he lives most of his adult life.[1]
1637Publishes the Discourse on Method, together with the essays Dioptrics, Meteors and Geometry.[1] [2]
1641Publishes the Meditations on First Philosophy, with the Objections and Replies (2nd edition 1642).[1] [2]
1644Publishes the Principles of Philosophy, the synthesis of his metaphysics and natural philosophy.[1] [2]
1649Publishes The Passions of the Soul; accepts Queen Christina of Sweden's invitation and moves to Stockholm.[1]
11 February 1650Dies in Stockholm, after falling ill in the Swedish winter.[1] [4]
